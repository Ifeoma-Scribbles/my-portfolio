Where product thinking and QA meet

When a feature works, but the workflow doesn’t

SwiftEntry is an enterprise visitor-management system. I took it over at about 50% of the MVP and delivered Version 1.0 within six months, testing end-to-end workflows, supporting client acceptance testing and working with engineering before deployment.

  1. 1

    I noticed

    Some workflows were more cumbersome than they needed to be, even though the features themselves worked.

  2. 2

    I asked

    I raised it with my manager and got approval to investigate. Then I gathered feedback from front-desk staff and an administrator to see where the experience was getting in the way.

  3. 3

    We improved

    I worked with the developers on selected workflow improvements for staff, administrators and visitors. We focused on selected workflows, not everything at once.

  4. 4

    I followed up

    After adoption, I ran follow-up sessions with front-desk and admin users to understand low routine use.

Testing should go beyond checking whether a feature works. It should also help us understand how the product works for the people using it.

Chapter 02

Quality Assurance

I test how a product behaves in practice—not just whether a feature appears to work. I look for gaps in workflows, unclear behaviour, and issues that could affect access or use.

The kinds of testing, in plain English

Exploratory
Using the product with no script first, to learn how it works and spot what looks off.
Manual
Following written test cases by hand, step by step, and recording what happens.
Regression
Re-checking what already worked after a fix, to make sure nothing else broke.
API
Checking the messages the product’s parts send each other, not just the screens. NuloAfrica.
Load
Seeing how the product behaves as more and more people use it at once. Naderkela.
Light security
A quick automated scan for common weaknesses. It is a first look, not a full security audit. Naderkela.
SEO
Checking how easily search engines can read and rank the pages. Naderkela.

Naderkela

A healthcare and telehealth platform with an integrated marketplace for patients.

  • Exploratory
  • Manual
  • Regression
  • Load (JMeter)
  • Light security (OWASP ZAP)
  • SEO (Lighthouse)
The product
The platform has separate workflows for administrators, doctors, patients and support agents, plus a marketplace.
My role
Freelance QA tester. I tested the full patient journey, from booking an appointment to a live video consultation, and the other roles around it.
What I tested
  • Admin staff management and oversight.
  • Doctor schedules, appointments, accepting or rejecting bookings, video consultations and article publishing.
  • The patient appointment journey.
  • Marketplace product flows, inventory and product management, and a glasses-recommendation flow based on what the patient enters.
How I worked
  1. The developer onboarded me to the platform.
  2. I started with exploratory testing to understand the product and its workflows.
  3. I wrote test cases and test sheets to track the work and the results.
  4. I tested the workflows manually.
  5. I used Apache JMeter to load-test the platform from 500 up to 5,000 concurrent users. At 5,000 I documented a stability failure and shared the findings with engineering.
  6. I ran a light security check with OWASP ZAP. This was an initial scan, not a full security audit.
  7. I used Lighthouse to check SEO.
  8. I re-tested fixes (regression) and followed up with the developers.
Status
This is an ongoing freelance engagement that began in June 2026.

NuloAfrica

A rental platform for landlords and tenants.

  • Exploratory
  • Manual
  • API
  • Regression
  • Test tracking
My role
Freelance QA tester. I designed and ran the testing and followed up with the developers.
What I did
  • Designed and ran a test workbook of 140+ test cases across 14 feature areas, covering sign-in, onboarding, payments, messaging and account roles.
  • Started with exploratory testing, then tested manually from written test cases and tracked every result in test sheets.
  • Tested the APIs, and checked payments end to end, including failed payments and error states.
  • Re-tested after fixes and followed up with the developers.
  • Traced a property-deletion failure to a backend data-structure problem, giving engineering the exact point of failure.
A finding worth noting
I found and documented a high-priority role-based access issue: an account could briefly reach an area meant for a different role.
